Single-Stage Spur Gearbox Design

Fall 2025 · Mechanical Power Transmission & Component Design

01 / Project Overview

As part of a three person team, I contributed to the engineering design of a single stage spur gearbox required to transmit 4 horsepower at 2000 rpm under light shock loading while achieving a target gear ratio of 4.5:1 (±2%). The objective was to develop a functional, manufacturable gearbox that satisfied performance, reliability, and safety requirements, including gear strength, shaft fatigue, and bearing life. I primarily used Autodesk Inventor and SolidWorks throughout the design process.

02 / Preliminary Design & Gear Selection

I began by breaking the gearbox into its main components: gears, shafts, bearings, and the enclosure. Before developing detailed CAD models, I created a preliminary hand sketch to plan the arrangement of the components, shaft alignment, and overall assembly.

I then evaluated gear geometry, including tooth count, diametral pitch, and the required reduction ratio. Several configurations were considered before selecting a 21 tooth pinion and 95 tooth gear, producing a final ratio of approximately 4.52:1, within the specified tolerance. The intermediate CAD models and tooth-meshing calculations helped evaluate gear engagement as the design developed.

03 / CAD Development & Design Verification

Following the preliminary design, I developed the gearbox assembly in Autodesk Inventor, including the enclosure, spur gears, shafts, and bearing arrangements. I evaluated multiple gear configurations before finalizing the required reduction ratio and component geometry. Using Inventor's Spur Gear Component Generator, I also performed gear strength calculations to verify that the selected design satisfied the specified operating requirements.

To account for light shock loading, I applied an overload factor of 1.5, increasing the design power from 4 to 6 horsepower. AGMA-based bending and contact stress calculations were used to evaluate the gear design. The strength calculation indicated design compliance, with minimum factors of safety of approximately 2.39 for contact and 3.50 for bending.

04 / Shaft & Bearing Design

With the gear geometry established, I designed the input and output shafts to accommodate transmitted torque, gear loads, and bearing locations. I evaluated the forces acting on each shaft and considered combined bending and torsional stresses, as well as features such as keyways and retaining ring grooves, when determining the required dimensions.

I then calculated the bearing reaction forces and evaluated the required static and dynamic load ratings. Bearing life calculations were based on a 4,000 hour operating requirement and 97.5% reliability, ensuring that the selected bearings could support the expected loads over the required service life.

05 / Engineering Drawings & Final Results

After completing the component design and calculations, I prepared detailed engineering drawings for the gears and shafts. These drawings document the component geometry, dimensions, and manufacturing details required to communicate the final design.

The final gearbox achieved a 4.52:1 reduction ratio within the required 4.5:1 ±2% specification. This project strengthened my understanding of mechanical power transmission, gear sizing, shaft loading, bearing selection, and translating engineering calculations into a complete CAD assembly. Working alongside an engineering team also strengthened my ability to coordinate design decisions and develop a reliable, manufacturable gearbox that met the project requirements.
